## 1. France: PAP.fr, Leboncoin and other sites, the share of sales without an agent, and why it is high (notaire, compromis, Hoguet/Alur, commissions)

### Takeaway
France is the clearest example in Europe of a large direct-sale market. About one third of resale transactions are often cited as happening without an agent, and that share has been stable since about 2000. It rests on two things. First, the mandatory **notaire** does the legally risky work (the authentic deed, title and registry checks, holding funds in escrow). Second, agent fees are high (usually 3–10%, about 5% on average). Leboncoin is the volume leader for private listings (about 71% of private sale listings, July 2025). PAP is the historic specialist brand with a smaller share (about 9.5%).

**Share of sales without an agent**
- A commonly cited figure, attributed to notaires: **"more than a third" of transactions escape agents, stable since 2000** (search snippet; the exact page was not confirmed). **[unverified]** — see [Reassurez-moi guide](https://reassurez-moi.fr/guide/immobilier/vendre-sans-agence) and [Pretto, "Acheter entre particuliers"](https://www.pretto.fr/acheteur-immobilier/achat-immobilier-particulier/vendre-particulier-a-particulier/)
- The IGEDD (ministry) analysis of agency market share (Friggit) says the agencies' share rose about 1 pt a year to **exceed 65% in the mid-2000s, then declined**, while direct sales between individuals grew. The PDF returned 403, so this is from the snippet. **[older]** — [IGEDD PDF](https://www.igedd.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/part-marche-agence-immobiliere_cle76e941.pdf)
- An agency-network blog (Laforêt, undated) cites **70% of sales via an agency vs 28% via a particulier-à-particulier website**. The same source gives another split: 19% via private sites and 13% via notaires or other intermediaries. The scopes and dates are unclear. **[older/unverified]** — [Laforêt Nanterre blog](https://www.laforet.com/agence-immobiliere/nanterre/blog/actualite/70-des-ventes-immobilieres-passent-un-professionnel)
- Counterpoint from the agent side: an **iad** study (Feb 2026) says **85% of individuals selling without an agent reported difficulties**. — [MySweetImmo, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.mysweetimmo.com/2026/02/13/vente-immobiliere-sans-agence-85-des-vendeurs-en-difficulte-selon-iad/)

**Legal framework: agents, commissions and the notaire**
- Agents are regulated by the **Loi Hoguet (loi n° 70-9 du 2 janvier 1970)** and its décret n° 72-678 (20 July 1972). A written mandate is mandatory. Fees are **free (unregulated)** but must be displayed. Agents are paid only once the sale is completed by the notarial authentic deed. — [DGCCRF fiche "L'agent immobilier" (Aug 2023)](https://economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques-et-les-faq/lagent-immobilier-les-regles-qui-encadrent-la-profession)
- Advertising rules (fee transparency introduced in the Alur era): listings must show the **price "honoraires inclus"**. If the buyer pays part of the fee, it must be shown **as a % of the price excluding fees**. — [DGCCRF](https://economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques-et-les-faq/lagent-immobilier-les-regles-qui-encadrent-la-profession)
- Commission levels: Pretto (updated Oct 2026) says classic agencies charge **3–10%**, parking spaces 10–15%, and fixed-fee agencies **€1,000–5,000**. Example: 5% of €600,000 is €30,000. Another source puts the national average at about **5.78%** **[unverified]**. Fee scales are usually degressive. — [Pretto](https://www.pretto.fr/vente-immobiliere/vendre-avec-ou-sans-agence/agence-immobiliere-ou-pap/); [Magnolia guide](https://www.magnolia.fr/guide-immobilier/agent-immobilier-commission)
- Notaire's role: the **compromis de vente can be signed sous seing privé** (directly between the parties, without a notaire), but the **final authentic deed must be signed before a notaire**. The **séquestre** (deposit held by the notaire until the deed) is negotiable, typically 5–10% of the price. The final deed fees (émoluments) are regulated, while drafting the compromis is priced freely. — [PAP, "Le rôle du notaire"](https://www.pap.fr/vendeur/compromis-vente/le-role-du-notaire-dans-une-transaction-immobiliere/a2521); [PAP, "Qu'est-ce qu'un séquestre"](https://www.pap.fr/vendeur/compromis-vente/immobilier-quest-ce-quun-sequestre-chez-le-notaire/a25432); [SeLoger, "Qui paye le compromis entre particuliers"](https://edito.seloger.com/conseils-d-experts/vendre/paye-compromis-une-vente-entre-particuliers-article-17127.html)
- Mandatory diagnostics (DPE and others) are the seller's job even without an agent. A third-party blog puts the cost at about **€300–600** per pack (weak source). **[unverified]** — [Qoridor blog](https://blog.qoridor.fr/article/vendre-sa-maison-2026-guide-complet)

### Inferences
- The French model works because the **notaire is a mandatory, neutral and liable professional**. The notaire checks title, easements, urbanisme and pre-emption rights, holds the funds and registers the sale. That leaves the agent mainly with marketing, price-setting and filtering buyers. A good portal (PAP or Leboncoin) can replace those functions, and a high commission (about 5% of the price) makes the saving worth thousands of euros.
- PAP earns money only from sellers and landlords (listings plus services such as coaching), and access is free for buyers and tenants. Leboncoin won the volume because it is free or cheap and has a huge general-marketplace audience. PAP now holds about 10% of private listings and competes on brand trust, content and guidance.
- The share of sales without an agent has been flat at about 30–35% for decades. The way it is measured (agency share of notarial transactions) means the figure probably includes sales between relatives and sales arranged through notaires.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) **official notaires-de-France statistic** on the share of transactions with and without an agent was found. The "1/3, stable since 2000" claim could not be traced to a primary page in this session.
- **PAP's listing price grid** (euros per listing or formula) was not retrievable: the pages returned 403 and the CGV give no figures.
- No exact current figures for PAP listing volume, or for Leboncoin's private vs professional split in real estate.
- Leboncoin's ownership was **not verified** here. From memory, it belongs to Adevinta, which Permira and Blackstone took private in 2024; check this. The status of the FNAIM lawsuit against PAP ([francetransactions](https://www.francetransactions.com/actus/immobilier/marche-immo/immobilier-la-fnaim-saisit-la-justice-contre-le-site-pap.html)) is unknown and undated.
- The regulated notary émolument rate for deeds (about 0.799% on the bracket above €60k, from memory) was **not confirmed** on Légifrance or notaires.fr.

## 2. Germany: ImmoScout24, Kleinanzeigen and Immowelt; Bestellerprinzip (2015), the 2020 commission-split law, Notar/Grundbuch, and the share of private sales

### Takeaway
Germany has a large private-listing segment but agents dominate. On ImmoScout24, **65% of condo listings in the 10 largest cities were posted by agents in Q4 2025**, so about 35% were private. The low point was 61% (Q4 2024). Two laws moved the market toward direct deals. The 2015 **Bestellerprinzip** for rentals cut the share of landlords using brokers from about two thirds to about one third. The 2020 **commission-split law** raised the share of commission-free sale offers from **35% to 43%** (IW, 2021). Mandatory notarial certification and the Grundbuch make a private sale legally safe without an agent.

### Cited Findings

**ImmoScout24 (Scout24 SE)**
- Maklerquote: **65%** of condominiums (Eigentumswohnungen) on ImmoScout24 in the **10 largest cities** were listed by agents in **Q4 2025**. It was **67% in Q3 2025** (the year's high) and **61% in Q4 2024** (the low). By city in Q4 2025: Leipzig 75%, Stuttgart 71%, Essen 70%, Hamburg 69%, Berlin 67%, Düsseldorf 66%, München 60%, Köln 58%, Frankfurt 58%, Dortmund 57%. Köln was only **41% in Q4 2024**. Method: **441,076 condos, 1 Jan 2020–31 Dec 2025**, analysed with Sprengnetter (a Scout24 company since 2023). — [ImmoScout24 press release, 15 Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.scout24.com/fileadmin/user_upload/ImmoScout24_Maklerquote_Q4-2025.pdf)
- Reach: "about **19 million users** search each month" on ImmoScout24 (boilerplate, Jan 2026). — [same PDF](https://www.scout24.com/fileadmin/user_upload/ImmoScout24_Maklerquote_Q4-2025.pdf)
- Scout24 FY2025 (reported Feb/Mar 2026): group revenue **€649.6M (+14.7%; organic +11.0%)**. **Private segment revenue +14.5%**. Private **subscription revenue +18.8% to €107.2M**, driven by "Plus" products. **Average private customers +14.0% to 506,937**. Fraudulent listings fell 19%. Professional segment +14.8%. — [Scout24 IR, preliminary FY2025 results](https://www.scout24.com/en/investor-relations/financial-news/ir-news/detail/scout24-publishes-preliminary-results-for-2025-and-provides-guidance-for-2026-confirming-growth-and-strong-operating-performance); [Online Marketplaces summary](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/scout24-fy2025-double-digit-revenue-growth-as-impending-acquisitions-adjust-2026-guidance/)
- Q1 2026: Private segment revenue **+8.8%**, driven by subscriptions (+5.3%) and pay-per-ad. — [Scout24 Q1 2026 news](https://www.scout24.com/en/news-media/news/detail/scout24-startet-stark-in-das-jahr-2026-zweistelliges-wachstum-margensteigerung-erhoehung-des-aktienrueckkaufvolumens-fuer-2026)
- Private-lister pricing: a **free basic listing (€0) for 14 days with up to 10 contact requests**, limited to **one free listing per 6 months**. After that, basic listings cost money. Prices depend on category (rent, buy, commercial, investment), duration (14 days, 1 month, 3 months), location and price, and **are shown only in the booking flow**. Premium packages are offered for more visibility. — [ImmoScout24 private listers page](https://www.immobilienscout24.de/anbieten/private-anbieter/immobilie-vermieten.html); [ImmoScout24 consumer information/prices](https://www.immobilienscout24.de/agb/verbraucherinformationen.html)

**Kleinanzeigen (formerly eBay Kleinanzeigen)**
- The real-estate category gets **more than 100M visits per month** and is one of the platform's most popular categories (VON POLL press release, Apr 2026). — [VON POLL / Kleinanzeigen cooperation PDF, 7 Apr 2026](https://cdn.von-poll.com/_dlpdf/260407_VPI_PM_Digi_Koop_Kleinanzeigen_VPI_FIN.pdf)
- A 2026 trade article reports new project pages for new-build developers, "Top-" and "Power-Anzeigen" formats for commercial listers, and **12,000+ real-estate professionals** on the platform. — [retail-news.de, 2026](https://retail-news.de/neubauvermarktung-kleinanzeigen-2026/)
- Real-estate ad volume grew **about 25% in one year** (konii headline, March 2024). **[older]** — [konii.de tag page](https://www.konii.de/news/tag/kleinanzeigen-immobilien/)

**Legal changes**
- **Bestellerprinzip (rentals):** since **1 June 2015**, whoever orders the broker pays, which in practice means the landlord. According to DIW (cited by Haufe), **about two thirds (62%) of landlords used an external broker before the reform, and about one third after**. A DIW Econ evaluation commissioned by the government found the goal "essentially achieved". — [Haufe, "Bestellerprinzip: Mieter sparen, Makler zahlen drauf"](https://www.haufe.de/immobilien/wirtschaft-politik/wohnungsvermittlungsgesetz-wovermrg_84342_548382.html); [DIW study page](https://www.diw.de/de/diw_01.c.832671.de/s_13337.html); [Bundestag Drucksache 19/31795](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/19/317/1931795.pdf)
- **Gesetz über die Verteilung der Maklerkosten** (dated **12 June 2020, BGBl. 2020 I 1245**; in force **23 Dec 2020**). It added **§§ 656a–656d BGB** for brokered purchases of **apartments and single-family homes** by consumers. The commission must be split equally if both sides hire the broker. If only the seller hires the broker, at most **50%** can be passed on to the buyer, and only once the seller has paid. — [IHK Wiesbaden](https://www.ihk.de/wiesbaden/bildung/berufszulassung/versicherung-finanzanlagen-immobiliendarlehen/immobilienmakler-und-wohnimmobilienverwalter/gesetz-ueber-die-verteilung-von-maklerkosten-4815540); [IHK Schwaben](https://www.ihk.de/schwaben/produktmarken/branchen-und-netzwerke/immobilienwirtschaft/gesetz-ueber-die-verteilung-von-maklerkosten-4986170); [Bundestag Drucksache 19/15827 (draft)](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/19/158/1915827.pdf); statute: [§ 656c BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__656c.html), [§ 656d BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__656d.html)
- **Effect (IW, 14 Jul 2021):** buyer commission fell by **1.5 pts on average** (from **5% to just under 3.5%**) by the end of Q1 2021. The share of property offers **without broker involvement or commission rose from 35% to 43%**, up +16 pts in Hamburg and +12 pts in Hesse. Total commission (buyer plus seller) in Germany is **just under 7%**, compared with **2% in the Netherlands** and **4% in Denmark**. IW notes that "in many markets with low commission rates, the share of broker-assisted sales is very high". **[older, 2021]** — [iwd (IW), "Geteilte Kosten bei der Maklerprovision", 14 Jul 2021](https://iwd.de/pdf/geteilte-kosten-bei-der-maklerprovision-514906)
- In practice the split is usually **50:50**, according to Haufe's review two years in. — [Haufe, "Zwei Jahre Provisionssplit"](https://www.haufe.de/immobilien/wirtschaft-politik/maklerrecht-bestellerprinzip-beim-kauf-von-immobilien_84342_489726.html)
- The agents' association (IVD) put the broker market share of residential purchases at **75% nationwide for Jan–May 2023**. It argues the real share is higher, because many properties are first offered privately and handed to a broker later. — [IVD Süd press release 57/2023 (PDF)](https://ivd-sued.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/2023-PN-57-Makler-Marktanteil-Deutschland.pdf)

**Notar and Grundbuch**
- A contract to transfer or acquire land **requires notarial certification** (notarielle Beurkundung) under § 311b(1) BGB. Ownership passes only on registration in the **Grundbuch**. Notary fees, together with Grunderwerbsteuer and the broker commission, make up the purchase side costs (Erwerbsnebenkosten). Statute text was not fetched in this session. — [§ 311b BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__311b.html); context in [iwd 2021](https://iwd.de/pdf/geteilte-kosten-bei-der-maklerprovision-514906)

### Inferences
- In Germany the **Notar plays the same safety role as the French notaire**. The contract is drafted and read out by a neutral notary, who also handles the Auflassungsvormerkung, the Grundbuch registration and usually the payment sequencing. Legally, a private sale is therefore as safe as a brokered one. The barriers are marketing reach and pricing, not legal risk.
- Germany's direct-deal share depends on policy. Moving commission costs onto the side that hires the broker (the landlord in 2015, about half to the seller in 2020) raised the share of offers without a broker by several points. The ImmoScout24 series, however, shows agents recovering to 65–67% in 2025 as markets became more complex (energy renovation, price discovery).
- ImmoScout24 earns money **from both sides of the private market**. Owners pay for listings and visibility after the one free listing every 6 months. Seekers pay for "Plus" subscriptions (MieterPlus/KäuferPlus-type products). This product mix is my reading of the "Plus products" description and was not verified line by line. The model is very different from France, where buyers pay nothing.

### Gaps
- No **official nationwide statistic** (Destatis, Gutachterausschüsse, Bundesnotarkammer) on the share of sales without a broker was found. The available figures are portal listing shares (ImmoScout24, IW) or industry estimates (IVD), and they cover different scopes.
- Exact **ImmoScout24 private package prices** in euros are not public. They are shown only in the booking flow.
- **Immowelt** was not researched in this session (owner, private-listing policy, traffic). From memory, it belongs to AVIV Group (Axel Springer/KKR); check this.
- Kleinanzeigen's private vs commercial real-estate split and its ownership were not verified. Snippets say private real-estate ads are free, but this was not confirmed on a Kleinanzeigen page.

## 3. Netherlands: Funda (agent-dominated) vs private sale options; share of sales without an agent

### Takeaway
The Dutch market is dominated by agents. The main portal, **Funda**, is reached through makelaars, including cheap "internet makelaars". Private sellers use smaller sites (Jaap, Huislijn, Huispedia, Zelfverkopen) or flat-fee online agents. Commissions are low (about 1.5–2%), which shrinks the incentive to sell yourself. No reliable national figure for sales without an agent was found.

### Cited Findings
- An SNS Bank guide for people selling themselves lists **jaap.nl, huislijn.nl, makelaarsland.nl and zelfverkopen.nl** as sites where individuals can advertise. It notes that an **internet makelaar can list on Funda but costs less** because the seller does much of the work. This means the cheap route onto Funda goes through a (flat-fee) agent, not a purely private listing. — [SNS e-book "Zelf je huis verkopen"](https://snsbank.nl/particulier/support/download-tonen-op-pagina/e-book-zelf-je-huis-verkopen-2.html)
- Huispedia, a self-sale portal, claims **550+ homes sold per month via its site** (self-reported, undated). — [Huispedia](https://huispedia.nl/woning-zelf-verkopen)
- NVM agents sold **about 47,600 homes in Q4 2025**. NVM data covers only member-agent sales, so self-sales are invisible in it. — [NVM analysis Q4 2025 (PDF)](https://www.nvm.nl/media/21wj3eby/bijlage-1-nvm-analyse-woningmarkt-4e-kwartaal-2025.pdf)
- Commission: IW (2021) puts the total Dutch broker commission at about **2%**, against about 7% in Germany. **[older]** A Dutch agency puts the average courtage at about **1.5%**. — [iwd 2021](https://iwd.de/pdf/geteilte-kosten-bei-der-maklerprovision-514906); [nieuws.nl](https://nieuws.nl/algemeen/hogere-huizenprijs-makelaar-duur)
- In a Funda survey, **91%** of respondents named the sale price as the key success criterion, and sellers said they wanted more insight into costs. — [Funda voor makelaars](https://www.funda.nl/voormakelaars/artikel-makelaar/verkopers-willen-graag-meer-grip-en-inzicht-met-advies-van-hun-makelaar/)
- A competition study of the brokerage market exists (TPEdigitaal, "Verborgen gebreken: concurrentie op de markt van makelaarsdiensten") but was not read. — [TPEdigitaal PDF](https://www.tpedigitaal.nl/sites/default/files/bestand/verborgen_gebreken_concurrentie_op_de_markt_van_makelaarsdiensten.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Netherlands supports IW's point: **low commissions together with an agent-controlled main portal lead to high agent use**. Disruption came from flat-fee online agents (for example Makelaarsland) who sell access to Funda cheaply, more than from a PAP-style private portal.
- Like France and Germany, the Netherlands uses a civil-law notary for the transfer deed. That reduces legal risk, but here it has not produced a large direct market, because getting listed on Funda is the real bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No reliable **percentage of Dutch sales without a makelaar** was found (Kadaster, CBS or NVM). Funda's current rules for private sellers and Jaap/Huislijn pricing were also not confirmed.
- Funda's ownership (historically NVM-founded) and current market share were not verified in this session.
- The Dutch legal points (notariële leveringsakte, written purchase contract with a 3-day bedenktijd) come from memory and were not confirmed on wetten.overheid.nl.

## 4. Norway and Sweden: Finn.no private listings; Hemnet (agent-only) and its pricing controversy; how strongly agents dominate

### Takeaway
Agents dominate in both countries, but the platforms differ. **Finn.no (Norway) accepts private sale listings**, priced by area and asking price, though most sales still go through a megler, who also handles the legal settlement (oppgjør). **Hemnet (Sweden) and its rival Boneo accept agent listings only**. Hemnet's aggressive price rises (ARPL ×16.8 from 2015 to 2025) caused an agent and seller backlash, and its share of Swedish homes sold fell from 89% to 82% in 2025.

- In the Nordics, **no notary is required**. The **licensed broker does the legal and escrow work** (contract, settlement, registry). In Norway a private seller has to buy that from an advokat. - The Hemnet case shows the risk of an agent-only portal monopoly. It could raise prices roughly 17× in ten years, until agents (the only customers) defected to Booli and Boneo. Sellers had no direct route around agents.

- Hemnet's current list prices per package (SEK) for 2025–2026 were not retrieved, only the ARPL averages.
- The ownership of Finn (Schibsted / Vend) and Hemnet was not verified in this session.

## 5. Common pattern: which legal systems favour direct transactions, and why

### Takeaway
Direct owner-to-owner deals thrive when three conditions hold together. (1) **A mandatory, neutral legal professional (the Latin notary: notaire, Notar) handles title, contract, escrow and registration**, so the agent adds only marketing. (2) **Agent commissions are high** (France 3–10%, about 5%; Germany about 7% in total before 2020), so selling yourself saves a lot. (3) **The dominant portals accept private listings** (PAP, Leboncoin, ImmoScout24, Kleinanzeigen, Finn). Agents dominate when commissions are low and the agent also supplies the legal or settlement service (Sweden, Norway), or when agents control the main portal (Funda, Hemnet, Boneo).

### Cited Findings
- IW: Germany's total commission is about 7%, against about 2% in the Netherlands and 4% in Denmark. "In many markets with low commission rates, the share of broker-assisted sales is very high." **[2021]** — [iwd, 14 Jul 2021](https://iwd.de/pdf/geteilte-kosten-bei-der-maklerprovision-514906)
- France: about 1/3 of sales without an agent, stable since 2000 **[unverified attribution]**. The agencies' share peaked above 65% in the mid-2000s and then declined. — [IGEDD](https://www.igedd.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/part-marche-agence-immobiliere_cle76e941.pdf); [Reassurez-moi](https://reassurez-moi.fr/guide/immobilier/vendre-sans-agence)
- Germany: about 35% private condo listings on ImmoScout24 in the top 10 cities (Q4 2025). Offers without a commission rose from 35% to 43% after the 2020 law. — [ImmoScout24, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.scout24.com/fileadmin/user_upload/ImmoScout24_Maklerquote_Q4-2025.pdf); [iwd 2021](https://iwd.de/pdf/geteilte-kosten-bei-der-maklerprovision-514906)
- Policy that shifts costs onto whoever hires the broker reduces broker use. The German rental Bestellerprinzip cut landlord broker use from about 62% to about 1/3. — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/immobilien/wirtschaft-politik/wohnungsvermittlungsgesetz-wovermrg_84342_548382.html)
- Sweden: agent-only portals hold 82–89% of homes sold (Hemnet, 2024–2025), and there is no private listing route. — [Online Marketplaces, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/if-youre-strong-you-have-to-be-nice-how-hemnet-lost-swedens-listings/)
- Norway: private sales are considered safe only if an advokat or megler handles the settlement. — [Dagbladet](https://www.dagbladet.no/nyheter/trygt-a-selge-boligen-privat/64931592)

### Inferences
- **Revenue models differ.** PAP charges sellers and landlords only (paid listings plus services such as coaching). ImmoScout24 charges both sides: owners after one free listing every 6 months, and seekers through Plus subscriptions. Leboncoin and Kleinanzeigen use a freemium model with cheap or free private listings, where the money comes from professionals and upsells. Hemnet and Finn charge per listing according to price and area, paid by the seller through the agent.
- **Rough ranking of how common direct deals are:** France (about 30%+ of transactions) > Germany (about 25–45% of listings depending on source; lower for actual transactions) >> Netherlands, Norway and Sweden (small; no reliable figures).
- For a Korean comparison, the notary-style "neutral legal safety layer" looks like the key enabler. Where the state or a notary guarantees title, contract and escrow, a direct-listing portal can take a large share. Without it, agents keep the transaction because they sell legal safety, not only listings.

### Gaps
- No cross-country academic or official dataset comparing the share of transactions without an agent was found. The comparison relies on portal and industry figures with different scopes (listings vs transactions, condos vs all homes, big cities vs nationwide).
- Southern European Latin-notary systems (Spain, Italy) and Denmark (low commission, high agent share) were out of scope or not researched.
