# North America (US & Canada): Direct-Transaction / FSBO / Low-Agent Real Estate Platforms

*Notes as of 2026-11-10. Every figure is dated. "(older)" flags data from before 2024. Where only a secondary or interested source (competitor blog, review site) exists, it is labelled.*

---

## 1. What share of US home sales are FSBO, and how has it changed?

### Takeaway
By NAR's survey, FSBO has fallen from a peak of about 21% (1985) to a record low of 5% in both the 2025 and 2026 Profiles. Agent use hit a record 92% in 2026. A large share of what is left is not "market" FSBO: many of these sellers sold to a friend, relative or neighbour. I found no strong non-NAR national estimate.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 Profile (released Nov 5–6, 2026; covers sales July 2025–June 2026):** FSBO "remained at a historic low of just 5% of sellers". 92% of sellers used an agent/broker, an all-time high. Median seller tenure was 12 years, also an all-time high. The NAR takeaways page as summarized also gives "62%" for FSBO sellers who knew the buyer. I could not check how NAR defines that metric, so verify it in the full report. — [NAR, Top 10 Takeaways 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/top-10-takeaways-from-nars-2026-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers); release and survey details (sent July 2026 to 228,250 recent buyers): [NAR newsroom](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-inches-up-to-22-median-age-remains-at-record-high-of-40); [WAV Group, Nov 5, 2026](https://www.wavgroup.com/2026/11/05/five-remarkable-trends-from-nars-2026-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/)
- **2025 Profile (Nov 2025; covers July 2024–June 2025):** FSBO 5%, described as a record low. 91% of sellers used an agent, a record. Median sale price was $360,000 for FSBO vs $425,000 for agent-assisted (an 18% gap). About 30% of FSBO sellers sold to a friend, relative or neighbour. — [NAR, "FSBOs Reach All-Time Low", Nov 11, 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents); [Virginia REALTORS summary, Dec 8, 2025](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/)
- NAR's article says FSBO homes are more often lower-cost mobile homes or rural properties, which may explain part of the price gap. It also says 40% of FSBO sellers did not actively market their home. — [NAR, Nov 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents)
- **2024 Profile (Nov 2024; covers July 2023–June 2024):** FSBO 6% per the report text, but NAR's press release said 7%. That is an internal NAR inconsistency. 90% of sellers used an agent, up from 89% in 2023. Median price was $380,000 for FSBO vs $435,000 for agent-assisted. Top FSBO reasons were selling to a relative, friend or neighbour (38%) and not wanting to pay a commission (30%). — [Atlanta Agent Magazine, Nov 15, 2024](https://atlantaagentmagazine.com/2024/11/15/nar-2024-profile-homesellers-fsbos/); [NAR 2024 Top 10 Highlights](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/the-top-10-highlights-from-nars-2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers); [Real Estate News, Nov 8, 2024](https://www.realestatenews.com/2024/11/08/typical-seller-is-older-than-ever-gets-full-asking-price)
- **Long-run trend:** FSBO peaked at up to 21% in 1985, has been mostly single digits since 2010, and has declined gradually over four decades. NAR's chart covers 1981–2025. — [NAR, Nov 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents)
- **Non-NAR survey (Clever Real Estate, cited by NAR):** nearly 30% of FSBO sellers struggled with pricing. 64% did not get their desired price. 43% admitted legal mistakes. About 1 in 5 eventually hired an agent. Most still paid a buyer's-agent commission, often as a seller concession. — [NAR citing Clever, Nov 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents)

### Inferences
- NAR's FSBO number is a survey of buyers and sellers by a trade group with an interest in the result, and it counts sales to acquaintances. The "market" FSBO share, meaning arm's-length sales to strangers through public marketing, is probably well under 5%.
- The 2024→2025→2026 readings (6%→5%→5%) show no FSBO rebound after the August 2024 commission rule changes.

### Gaps
- I found no independent national FSBO share estimate (from Census, HUD, ATTOM or similar) for 2024–2026 to check NAR's figure against. Census and HUD do not track FSBO.
- The full NAR annual series (1981–2026) is only in a chart and not as text. Exact values for each year in 2010–2023 were not extracted.
- The 2026 FSBO vs agent median prices were not on the takeaways page.

## 2. What changed after the NAR commission settlement (Sitzer/Burnett), and did FSBO or flat-fee models grow?

### Takeaway
The rules changed. Offers of buyer-broker compensation were banned from MLSs, and written buyer agreements became required, both effective Aug 17, 2024. Prices largely did not: Redfin's average buyer-agent commission went *up* from 2.36% (Q3 2024) to 2.42% (Q3 2025), and FSBO stayed at a record-low 5% in 2025 and 2026. I found no hard data showing a measurable rise in flat-fee or discount market share.

### Cited Findings
- **Verdict and settlement:** On Oct 31, 2023, a jury in *Sitzer/Burnett* returned a $1.78B verdict, which could have been trebled to more than $5B. NAR settled for $418M paid over about 4 years. Final court approval came Nov 26, 2024 (W.D. Mo., Judge Stephen Bough). — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/realtors-get-final-approval-of-418-million-antitrust-settlement); [Chicago Agent, Nov 26, 2024](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2024/11/26/nar-lawsuit-settlement-receives-final-approval/); [Inman, Nov 26, 2024](https://inman.com/2024/11/26/court-grants-final-approval-to-nars-landmark-settlement-deal)
- **Appeal:** On Aug 19, 2026, an Eighth Circuit panel upheld the Sitzer/Burnett settlements. — [Real Estate News, Aug 19, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/19/appeals-court-upholds-settlements-in-sitzer-burnett); [Florida Realtors, Aug 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/08/appeals-court-rules-nar-sitzer-burnett-settlement-remains-intact)
- **Practice changes effective Aug 17, 2024:**
  - Offers of compensation are prohibited on MLSs. Sellers can still offer compensation off-MLS and can offer buyer concessions on the MLS.
  - Buyers must sign a written agreement with their agent before touring.
  - Realtor-owned MLSs had to implement the changes by that date.
  - Sources: [NAR final reminder press release](https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/national-association-of-realtors-provides-final-reminder-of-august-17-nar-practice-change-implementation); [NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/competition-in-real-estate/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers); [NAR settlement FAQ (Oct 29, 2024)](https://nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2024-10/nar-settlement-faq-2024-10-29.pdf)
- **Commissions after the change (Redfin data):**
  - The average buyer's-agent commission was 2.36% in Q3 2024 and 2.42% in Q3 2025. — [Inman, Dec 8, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/12/08/buyers-agent-commissions-see-rebound-in-wake-of-settlement/); [MPA Mag](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/industry-trends/buyer-agent-commissions-return-to-pre-settlement-levels-redfin/546199)
  - Q2 2025 was 2.43%, the third straight quarterly rise. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-agent-commissions-q2-2025-post-nar-settlement/)
  - In Feb 2025, Redfin reported that commissions had risen slightly for affordable homes and fallen slightly for expensive homes since the change. — [Business Wire / Redfin, Feb 4, 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250204920585/en/Redfin-Reports-Commissions-Have-Risen-Slightly-For-Affordable-Homes-Fallen-Slightly-For-Expensive-Homes-Since-NAR-Settlement-Took-Effect)
- **Interested-party framing:** Offerpad's content page (updated June 2026) is titled "Two Years After the Commission Shakeup, Real Estate Fees Went Up, Not Down". It is an iBuyer with a stake in this story. — [Offerpad](https://www.offerpad.com/articles/real-estate-commission-rates-2026/)
- **FSBO share after the settlement:** 5% in the 2025 Profile, the first survey covering post-Aug-2024 sales, and 5% again in 2026 with agent use at a record 92%. — [NAR 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents); [NAR 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/top-10-takeaways-from-nars-2026-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers)
- **Zillow's listing rules after the settlement:** On Apr 10, 2025, Zillow announced "Listing Access Standards", its implementation of NAR's Clear Cooperation Policy, effective May 2025. They bar agent listings that are publicly marketed but withheld from the MLS. FSBO, rental and certain builder listings are exempt. — [Real Estate News, Apr 26, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/04/26/zillow-gains-more-support-for-new-listing-policy); [Zillow Pro](https://www.zillow.com/pro/agents-know-listing-access-standards/)

### Inferences
- The settlement shifted the *mechanics* of buyer-agent pay, from MLS-advertised to negotiated or concessions-based, more than the *level* of pay. A FSBO seller now saves at most the listing side (about 3%), not the full combined fee, because buyers' agents still expect to be paid, often through seller concessions. That shrinks the economic case for FSBO.
- Competitors and flat-fee firms (Houzeo, Beycome, ByOwner) market heavily on "post-settlement" savings. Measured market-share data to support a shift is absent.

### Gaps
- I found no Redfin buyer-agent commission figure for any quarter of 2026.
- I found no reliable time series on flat-fee MLS or discount brokerage market share before and after the settlement. Third-party blogs claim growth but cite nothing.
- A Federal Reserve analysis (May 2025) reportedly concluded that buyer-representation requirements "may end up having no effect on commission rates". I could not locate the primary paper.

## 3. Main US models and examples (business model, pricing, scale, status)

### Takeaway
The US "direct" market splits into six layers:
1. Cheap FSBO listing portals (ForSaleByOwner.com, FSBO.com, ByOwner.com, Zillow's free FSBO posts)
2. Flat-fee MLS "entry-only" brokers (Houzeo and similar), the most practical FSBO route because the MLS still drives syndication
3. Discount or referral brokers (Clever; Redfin, now part of Rocket)
4. iBuyers (Opendoor, Offerpad), the "sell to a company" route. Opendoor is scaling acquisitions again but losing money. Zillow quit in 2021.
5. Direct rental marketplaces (Zillow Rentals, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist), where the landlord-direct model is far more common but scam-prone
6. Closing and transaction tech (Propy, Opendoor's Doma purchase)

### Cited Findings

**A. Pure FSBO listing sites**
- **ForSaleByOwner.com:**
  - Founded 1999 in New York by Damon Giglio, with Colby Sambrotto as COO. — [Institute for Justice](https://ij.org/client/damon-giglio-and-colby-sambrotto/)
  - Bought by Tribune Interactive in 2006. — [Inman, May 22, 2006](https://inman.com/2006/05/22/tribune-interactive-buys-forsalebyownercom) (older)
  - Bought in May 2018 by In-House Realty, a Rock Holdings / Quicken Loans affiliate, from Tronc for a reported $2.5M. Tronc said the site no longer fit its digital strategy. — [Inman, May 24, 2018](https://www.inman.com/2018/05/24/quickens-in-house-realty-purchases-forsalebyowner-com-for-2-5m/); [RISMedia, May 24, 2018](https://www.rismedia.com/2018/05/24/house-realty-acquires-forsalebyowner-com/)
  - Now within the Rocket family. — [Real Estate News, Oct 3, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/10/03/byowner-offers-fsbo-sellers-options-and-no-commissions)
  - The fall from a Tribune asset to a $2.5M sale shows how little standalone FSBO portals are worth.
- **FSBO.com:** a site of more than 20 years. Acquired in Feb 2026 by a group led by Mike Kortas (founder and CEO of NEXA Lending) with Brad Rice. Kortas said NEXA itself does not own FSBO.com. It was relaunched as an "agentic AI" marketplace. The price was not disclosed. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fsbo-com-acquisition-platform-upgrade/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/fsbocom)
- **ByOwner.com (Oct 2025):**
  - Bought in 2023 by Derek Eisenberg and Colby Sambrotto, a former ForSaleByOwner.com co-owner.
  - Three-part model: pure FSBO listings, flat-fee or discount MLS, and an in-house brokerage that refers sellers to agents.
  - Leads go to the listing originator. The owners criticize Zillow for selling ZIP-code leads to agents.
  - Pricing: $95 non-MLS listing, $195 MLS listing.
  - MLS brokerage in 46 states + DC (not yet in OK, WI, OR, MT).
  - Raising capital through a Reg CF offering. Also lists rentals. Sambrotto reported more tenant than buyer interest over the prior 12 months.
  - Source: [Real Estate News, Oct 3, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/10/03/byowner-offers-fsbo-sellers-options-and-no-commissions)
- **Zillow FSBO:**
  - Zillow still lets owners post FSBO listings for free in most areas.
  - Its support page says: "To comply with local real estate rules, Zillow can no longer assist in creating For Sale By Owner (FSBO) listings in your area". It then points users to an unaffiliated free site. — [Zillow FSBO Support](https://www.zillow.com/posting/for-sale-by-owner-support)
  - Wisconsin is the main example, with FSBO posting ended July 26, 2021, per a third-party law-firm blog. Some blogs also name Montana. — [Knoll Greller blog](https://www.knollgreller.com/post/why-you-can-t-list-fsbo-on-zillow-in-wisconsin-the-brokerage-trap) (secondary)
- **Zillow display history (FSBO visibility):**
  - In Jan 2021, Zillow switched to MLS IDX feeds and moved non-agent listings (FSBO, auctions) to a separate "Other listings" tab, hidden by default in the app. — [Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/update-on-zillow-listings)
  - Inman reported that pageviews for non-MLS listings "plummeted" under the two-tab setup.
  - From Dec 2024, Zillow began "commingling" FSBO again in a single search view, in cities where rules allow. — [Inman, Apr 22, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/04/22/listings-are-quietly-commingling-on-zillow-again-after-4-year-hiatus/); [OnlineMarketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/zillow-begins-rolling-back-its-two-tab-search-experience/)

**B. Flat-fee MLS services**
- **Houzeo:**
  - Tiered flat-fee MLS plans; Bronze is $399 for 6 months with $0 due at closing, per Houzeo's own pricing page. — [Houzeo pricing](https://houzeo.com/pricing)
  - Some reviewers report higher tiers adding a percentage at closing (e.g., Gold $299 upfront + 1%). These are third-party reviews, some by competitors. — [Bankrate review](https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/houzeo-review); [Beycome (competitor)](https://www.beycome.com/blog/houzeo-reviews-pros-cons/)
  - Operates in all 50 states + DC and syndicates to Zillow and Realtor.com through the MLS. Revenue comes from seller flat fees, a share of fees paid to partners, and paid ads for service providers. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1489315); [HomeLight review](https://www.homelight.com/blog/houzeo-reviews/)
  - A reported "$23M revenue in 2023" is unverified (review sites only).
- **Homie (Utah/Arizona, flat-fee brokerage):** a failure case.
  - Staff cut about 28% in early 2022, then shrank from about 600 to about 40 during 2023. CEO Johnny Hanna stepped down in Oct 2022. — [Salt Lake Tribune, Nov 9, 2023](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2023/11/09/with-layoffs-hitting-homie-whats/); [RealTrends](https://realtrends.com/articles/ceo-of-flat-fee-brokerage-homie-resigns-following-layoffs)
  - Shed its last agents in spring 2024. — [SL Tribune, Apr 19, 2024](https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2024/04/19/homie-utah-company-meant-disrupt/)
  - Sold in Feb 2025 (buyer not named in my sources). Now fewer than two dozen agents in UT/AZ, and Homie Loans was shuttered. — [Inman, May 19, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/05/19/once-on-a-path-for-expansion-homie-now-a-shell-of-its-former-self/)
  - Its antitrust suit against competitors was dismissed in July 2025; Homie appealed in Aug 2025. — [SL Tribune, Aug 22, 2025](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2025/08/22/utah-real-estate-homie-appeals/)

**C. Discount / hybrid / referral brokers (brief)**
- **Clever Real Estate:**
  - A referral network, not a traditional brokerage. Sellers pay a 1.5% listing fee ($3,000 minimum) at closing. Buyer-agent pay is separate and negotiable.
  - Clever earns a referral fee from the partner agent only when a deal closes. — [Clever FAQ](https://www.listwithclever.com/real-estate-faqs/)
  - Launched at 1%. Raised an $8M Series B in Dec 2021, for $13.5M total. — [Inman, Dec 1, 2021](https://www.inman.com/2021/12/01/clever-real-estate-raises-8m-in-series-b-eyes-mortgages/); [Inman, Jun 5, 2019](https://www.inman.com/2019/06/05/full-service-for-1-percent-clever-real-estate-gets-3-point-5-million-in-funding/) (older)
- **Redfin:**
  - Acquired by Rocket Companies in an all-stock deal ($1.75B, $12.50/share) that closed Jul 1, 2025. Rebranded "Redfin Powered by Rocket". Glen Kelman stayed on, reporting to Rocket CEO Varun Krishna.
  - Launched "Rocket Preferred Pricing": a 1-point rate cut for the first year or up to a $6,000 lender credit for buyers using a Redfin agent or listing plus Rocket Mortgage.
  - Rocket expects more than $200M in synergies.
  - Sources: [Real Estate News, Jul 1, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/07/01/rocket-finalizes-usd1-75b-acquisition-of-redfin); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/rocket-companies-completes-redfin-acquisition-new-pricing-program-is-first-perk-of-partnership/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/rocket-completes-redfin-deal-announces-rocket-preferred-pricing/)
  - With ForSaleByOwner.com (Rocket) plus Redfin plus Rocket Mortgage, one lender-led group now owns a FSBO portal, a discount brokerage and a mortgage funnel.

  - Closed a $100M credit facility from Metropolitan Partners Group (announced Jan 29, 2026) to roll up licensed title and escrow firms with AI and blockchain closing tech. — [Crowdfund Insider, Jan 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/259040-proptech-firm-propy-secures-100m-credit-facility-to-enhance-real-estate-closing-process/); [FinTech Global, Feb 2, 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/02/02/propy-secures-100m-facility-to-automate-real-estate-closings/)
  - About $102.7M raised in total over 8 rounds; acquired a title business (Boss Law) on Mar 12, 2026. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/propy)
  - The pivot is toward *owning licensed title agencies*, not bypassing them.
- **Opendoor + Doma:** Opendoor completed its acquisition of Doma (closing/escrow) in Q3 2026. — [Real Estate News, Nov 6, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/06/opendoor-behind-schedule-on-profitability-goal-as-losses-mount)

### Inferences
- The surviving US "direct" models have **re-attached to the MLS or to a licensed intermediary** rather than replaced them: flat-fee MLS entry, ByOwner's brokerage arm, Propy buying title agencies, and Opendoor buying Doma and adding a mortgage business. Pure listing portals are low-value assets ($2.5M ForSaleByOwner.com sale in 2018).
- Monetization is moving from listing fees to **adjacent financial services**: mortgage (Rocket/Redfin, Opendoor), title and escrow (Propy, Doma), and referral fees (Clever, ByOwner).
- Opendoor's ~2,700 homes sold per quarter (about 11k annualized) is a tiny fraction of the US resale market. The iBuyer model remains a niche liquidity product.
- Rentals are where landlord-direct listing is mainstream, and Zillow is monetizing it (+39% revenue in 2025). Fraud is the main friction.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable listing or transaction counts for ForSaleByOwner.com, FSBO.com or Houzeo, and no audited Houzeo revenue or funding figures.
- I found no Propy transaction volume for 2025–2026.
- Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace do not disclose rental listing volumes.
- I could not identify who bought Homie in Feb 2025.

## 4. Canada: DuProprio/ComFree, PropertyGuys.com and others

### Takeaway
Canada's FSBO center is **Quebec**, where DuProprio has long claimed about 20% of residential transactions. In 2024 its owner Desjardins would only say "between 10% and 20%". Ownership went from founders → Yellow Pages (2016) → Purplebricks (2018, C$51M) → Desjardins (2020, C$60.5M). Outside Quebec, FSBO is smaller, served by the PropertyGuys.com franchise network and formerly ComFree (about 2% of Ontario in 2018). Several "disruptor" brokerages failed (Purplebricks Canada → FairSquare, closed 2023; Properly, sold off in 2023).

## 5. Why does FSBO stay small in the US despite the tools?

### Takeaway
The binding constraints are **distribution and the other side of the deal, not software**. The MLS (and its syndication to Zillow and others) remains the main path to buyers. Most buyers are represented by agents who still expect pay, often from seller concessions. Pricing, disclosure and legal risk deter owners. And a large share of FSBO is non-arm's-length. Raw NAR price gaps overstate any agent premium. The most rigorous study (Madison, WI) found no price difference, though it was a single market.

### Cited Findings
- **MLS and portal distribution:**
  - Zillow's 2021–2024 two-tab design hid FSBO behind "Other listings", and non-MLS pageviews "plummeted". — [Inman, Apr 22, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/04/22/listings-are-quietly-commingling-on-zillow-again-after-4-year-hiatus/); [Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/update-on-zillow-listings)
  - Zillow cannot accept FSBO posts in some areas "to comply with local real estate rules". — [Zillow FSBO Support](https://www.zillow.com/posting/for-sale-by-owner-support)
- **Buyer-agent norms:** most FSBO sellers still paid a buyer's-agent commission, often as a concession (Clever survey). — [NAR, Nov 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents)
- **Buyer-agent pay levels:** pay rose after the settlement, so the agent side of the market was not disrupted (2.36%→2.42%, Q3 2024→Q3 2025). — [Inman, Dec 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/12/08/buyers-agent-commissions-see-rebound-in-wake-of-settlement/)
- **Pricing and legal burden:** nearly 30% of FSBO sellers struggled with pricing and relied on online estimators. 43% admitted legal mistakes. 47% said the process brought them to tears. About 20% ended up hiring an agent (Clever survey, as cited by NAR). — [NAR, Nov 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents)
- **NAR price gap and its caveats:**
  - Medians were $360k (FSBO) vs $425k (agent) in 2025, and $380k vs $435k in 2024.
  - NAR itself notes FSBO skews toward mobile homes and rural property.
  - About 30–38% of FSBOs are sales to friends, relatives or neighbours, which are often discounted.
  - Sources: [NAR 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents); [Atlanta Agent Magazine (2024)](https://atlantaagentmagazine.com/2024/11/15/nar-2024-profile-homesellers-fsbos/)
- **Academic counter-evidence (older):**
  - Hendel, Nevo & Ortalo-Magné, *AER* 2009 (99:5, 1878–98). They studied Madison, WI listings from 1998–2004, matched FSBOMadison.com data with Realtor MLS data and checked prices against assessor records.
  - Average prices were $175,068 (FSBO) vs $173,205 (MLS), with no significant difference. MLS listing *did* shorten time on market.
  - The FSBO listing cost $150, vs a 6% commission.
  - Caveats: seller self-selection, a single market, and a rising-price period.
  - Sources: [EconPapers (AER)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:99:y:2009:i:5:p:1878-98); [NBER w13360](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13360.pdf); [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/the_superfluousness_of_realtors)
- **Industry-side counter-study (older):** Collateral Analytics (Sklarz & Miller, Aug 2017), "Saving real estate commissions at any price?", found FSBO sales priced below MLS sales. The page now redirects to ICE Mortgage Technology, so I could not re-extract exact percentages. NAR is commonly cited as the funder. — [Collateral Analytics (archived URL, now redirects)](https://collateralanalytics.com/saving-real-estate-commissions-at-any-price/)

### Inferences
- The FSBO price-gap debate is largely about **selection**: what kind of property is sold and to whom. It is not clean causal evidence. The best quasi-experimental study (Madison) showed parity on price but slower sales.
- The cheapest workable US "direct" path is FSBO marketing plus a few-hundred-dollar flat-fee MLS entry. This confirms that MLS access, not the listing website, is the bottleneck.
- Post-settlement, the remaining saving from FSBO (about the listing side only) may not compensate many owners for time, risk and pricing uncertainty, which is consistent with FSBO at 5%.

### Gaps
- I did not extract exact figures from the Collateral Analytics 2017 study (the page redirects), and I did not verify its funding.
- I found no recent (2024–2026) peer-reviewed US study comparing FSBO and agent prices.
- State-by-state legal requirements were not compiled: seller disclosure forms, attorney-closing states, and Wisconsin's rules on non-broker listing on portals.

## 6. Known failures, lawsuits and pivots

### Takeaway
The space is littered with exits and pivots:
- Zillow Offers (2021, about $880M lost)
- Homie (from about 600 staff to a shell; its antitrust suit was dismissed in 2025)
- Purplebricks' US, Australian and Canadian exits (2019–2020)
- FairSquare closed (2023) and Properly broken up (2023)
- Opendoor's FTC case ($62M, 2022) and continued losses (–$218M in Q3 2026)
- FSBO portals sold for little (ForSaleByOwner.com, $2.5M in 2018) or re-sold (FSBO.com, 2026)

- **Pivots to licensed infrastructure:** Propy is buying title agencies with a $100M facility (2026). — [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/02/propy-secures-100m-facility-to-automate-real-estate-closings/). Opendoor bought Doma and grew mortgage originations. — [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/06/opendoor-behind-schedule-on-profitability-goal-as-losses-mount)

### Inferences
- The recurring failure mode is **balance-sheet risk** (iBuyers buying homes in volatile markets) or **unit economics of low-fee full service** (Homie, Purplebricks). Survivors either charge tiny fixed fees with minimal service (flat-fee MLS, FSBO portals) or sit inside a lender or financial group that monetizes the mortgage (Rocket, Desjardins).
- For a new direct-transaction platform, the North American evidence points to a narrower path: owning the closing and finance layer, or partnering with it, matters more than the listing layer.

### Gaps
- I did not research the Purplebricks US exit (2019) or Knock, EasyKnock and other US hybrids in detail.
- I found no data on FSBO-specific fraud or legal-dispute rates (e.g., disclosure lawsuits against FSBO sellers versus agent-represented sellers).
